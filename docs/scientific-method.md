# Scientific method

SIFTER decomposes a finite, one-dimensional spectrum into a constant baseline and one shared family of Gaussian, Lorentzian, or Voigt peaks. Every peak amplitude is integrated area. Gaussian width is standard deviation `sigma`; Lorentzian width is half-width at half-maximum `gamma`; Voigt peaks expose both.

The governing rule is: **Fourier domain informs the fit; original data determines the fit.** Fourier magnitude-envelope tendencies and candidate spacings can initialize or diagnose candidates, but they never replace the observed intensities in optimization or scoring. Ordinary FFT analysis fails closed on materially nonuniform grids unless diagnostic-only interpolation is explicitly enabled.

Candidates include simpler peak counts in automatic peak-count mode. Exact peak-count mode fits only the requested count. The interactive workbench caps guided fits at 10 peaks and merges detected maxima with user-clicked peak hints as strong proposal priors, not forced truths. Fast, Standard, Thorough, and Research modes screen bounded candidate pools before refining finalists. Windowed progressive fitting can propose starts from local cores and halos, but local scores and baselines are not exported as global evidence. Every recommended model is jointly evaluated and scored against the complete original spectrum with one global constant baseline.

The v0.4.2 workbench presents peak measurements first and orders retained candidate models by user-facing quality: severe fit notes, worst local area error, median height error, normalized RMSE, peak count, then BIC/AICc as advanced tie-breakers. Delta BIC expresses relative criterion distance, not a probability. Reduced chi-squared is reported only when positive measurement standard deviations are supplied.

Fast mode targets interactive use and may report `FAST_APPROXIMATE_FIT` when a bounded polishing budget is used after screening. Standard, Thorough, and Research modes spend more time on optimizer convergence and diagnostics. Fast uncertainty uses a local Jacobian covariance approximation and withholds intervals when rank or variance assumptions fail. Thorough uncertainty resamples residuals and refits the selected model. Structured warnings identify approximate fast fits, collapsed areas, near-bound parameters, extreme sensitivity correlation, truncated peaks, close centers, ambiguous selection, and unavailable uncertainty.

Peak overlap is allowed when resolved maxima retain defensible component ownership. A component spanning two resolved maxima is kept rankable but flagged as a shoulder or unresolved-doublet limitation. A component spanning more than two resolved maxima is inadmissible, even when the broad-band override is enabled.

Temperature, laser power, generic conditions, and trusted references are provenance or initialization aids only. They do not apply hidden heating, normalization, linewidth physics, or forced peak identity. Related-spectrum plots summarize independently fitted results by conservative center/FWHM matching and report ambiguity instead of fitting a global condition-dependent physical model.

SIFTER is validated on deterministic, identifiable synthetic cases and local private-data smoke checks. It makes no claim of super-resolution, technique-specific physics, causal peak assignment, or reliable recovery when the data do not identify a unique decomposition.
