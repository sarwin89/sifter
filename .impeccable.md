## Design Context

### Users

SIFTER serves experimental scientists who need to decompose one-dimensional spectra locally, often without writing code. Their core job is to load a CSV, TXT, or DAT spectrum, confirm the coordinate and intensity columns, run a conservative automatic analysis, inspect the recommended model against credible alternatives, understand limitations, and export a reproducible result without exposing experimental data.

### Brand Personality

Calm, rigorous, and instrument-like. The interface should build confidence through precise language, visible provenance, restrained hierarchy, and candid warnings. It should feel like dependable laboratory software: focused during setup, measured during analysis, and unambiguous when evidence is limited.

### Aesthetic Direction

Use a refined light-mode scientific-workbench aesthetic. Favor warm paper-like neutrals, deep ink, and a sparingly used mineral green accent; reserve amber and red for warnings and errors. Typography should be compact and highly legible with tabular numerals for measurements. Avoid neon-on-dark styling, decorative gradients, glass effects, generic card grids, and visual claims of certainty that the analysis does not support.

### Design Principles

1. Make the four-stage workflow obvious: load, map columns, configure, analyze.
2. Keep defaults simple and place computationally expensive or specialist controls behind progressive disclosure.
3. Put the recommendation beside its evidence: BIC, AICc, alternatives, residuals, uncertainty method, and warnings.
4. Never encode status by color alone; use concise labels, warning codes, and explanatory text.
5. Treat privacy and reproducibility as interface features: emphasize local operation, show the fixed seed, and generate exports only after explicit actions.
